TITLE: Cardiovascular and Pulmonary Clinical Guidelines
SOURCE: American College of Cardiology & AHA Clinical Standards
TOPIC: Electrocardiogram (ECG), Troponin, and Chest Pain Assessment

SUMMARY:
1. Normal resting heart rate for adults ranges between 60 and 100 beats per minute (bpm). Sinus tachycardia is defined as a sinus rhythm with a rate exceeding 100 bpm.
2. ST-segment elevation on a 12-lead ECG may indicate acute myocardial injury or infarction. Nonspecific ST-T wave changes require clinical correlation with cardiac biomarkers.
3. Serum Troponin I and T are highly sensitive biomarkers for myocardial infarction. An elevated troponin level indicates cardiac cell injury but must be interpreted in clinical context alongside symptoms and serial ECGs.
4. Ejection Fraction (EF) measures the percentage of blood pumped out of the left ventricle with each contraction. A normal LVEF is 55% to 70%.
5. Patient communication guideline: Medical reports noting elevated enzymes or abnormal ECG waves describe laboratory and electrical observations. Clinicians evaluate these findings in conjunction with physical examination and coronary history.
